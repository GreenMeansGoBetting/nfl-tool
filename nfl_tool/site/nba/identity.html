Tonight's Edges

Each card is something that favors that team tonight, biggest first, with the proof from both sides under it. Offense Vs Defense cards need the offense's number and the other defense's allowed number to agree (one clearly, a 0.6 league spread from average, and the other not pointing the other way). Opponent Type cards: tonight's opponent is a type this team plays far better or worse against than normal (3+ games). Schedule Spot cards: the same for a spot the team is in tonight. Big Edge = the two sides average a full league spread (or a type / spot gap far past the color line). Ranks are out of the 30 teams this season. The details are in the tables further down.

The Angles

How the game is likely to look, not who wins it. How often a team does something (pace, where it shoots) isn't good or bad on its own. Fits = the offense does it a lot and this defense gives it up a lot, so expect more of it (think overs on that player's props). Takes Away = the offense does it a lot and this defense rarely allows it, so expect less. Ranks are out of the 30 teams this season.

Matchup Extremes

What it asks: does this team play differently against a certain type of opponent (fast teams, 3-point teams, a defense that fouls...) than the rest of the league does? Top block = its defense against those offenses (Pts Allowed, 3s Allowed...). Bottom block = its offense against those defenses (Pts Scored, 3s Made...). Every number = how much further from its own normal it ran than the league does, a 100 possessions: +8 Pts Allowed = it gives up 8 more than you'd expect against these teams. Green = good for this team, red = bad; Strength / Weak Spot tags call out the points. Tonight = tonight's opponent is that type, so those rows are the ones that matter for this game. GP = games against that type; under 3 games is faded and never colored (one odd night isn't a pattern). Pace is never good or bad. Hover a number for the real numbers, a type name for its teams.

Schedule Spots

The same idea for the schedule: how each team does in a spot (back-to-backs, long road trips, altitude...) compared with its own games overall, next to how the whole league moves in that spot. Pts Scored / Pts Allowed a 100 possessions, each = how much further from its own normal it ran in that spot than the league does (green = good for this team, red = bad); pace isn't colored. Tonight = the team is in that spot in this game. GP = games in that spot this season; under 3 is faded and never colored. Rest Edge = 2+ more days off than the opponent; a road trip or home stand counts games in a row away or at home; National TV / NBA Cup = a national broadcast or a Cup game.

The Teams

Offense / Defense tags: up to four things each team does far more or less than the league (a full standard deviation or more), biggest first. 4th quarter: how often the team was up or down 15+ in the 4th, and how many of that night's starters were on the floor then on average (low = the coach empties the bench); "Within 5" is the same in close 4th quarters. By quarter: points scored minus allowed per 100 possessions in each quarter, colored against the league.

Offense Vs Defense

Every row reads both sides: what the offense does next to what this defense lets its opponents do. Green = good for that side, colored against the league; hover a number for its league rank. Mismatch / Tough = the two sides are a full standard deviation apart one way or the other; Good vs Good = both clearly good. How OFTEN a team does something (pace, shot shares) isn't good or bad, so it isn't colored: Fits = the offense does it a lot and this defense gives it up a lot; Takes away = the offense does it a lot and this defense rarely allows it. Transition = a shot or turnover within 8 seconds of a defensive rebound or a steal.

Data: sportsdataverse / hoopR (ESPN play-by-play), rebuilt into possessions with the ten players on the floor.
