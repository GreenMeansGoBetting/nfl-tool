Every game played 10,000 times, possession by possession, from this season's games. The books' line is never an input, only the price a play is judged against.

One game, a quarter at a time: the two teams' pace sets the possessions. Each possession goes to one of the five on the floor, by his minutes and how much he shoots when he's out there. His play is a shot from a zone (rim, short, mid-range, corner three, above-the-break three), a trip to the line or a turnover, by his own mix tilted toward what this defense gives up. Whether it goes in is his make rate there times what this defense allows there. A missed shot can come back as an offensive rebound. Down the stretch a blowout sends the starters to the bench, and a lead shrinks a little (the team ahead eases off). Every player also gets a minutes night and a shooting night, so the spread of outcomes is a real game's. Injuries and starters: a player listed Out leaves his minutes to the rest of the rotation; a player in the NBA's starting five tonight plays starter minutes. The dials are the spots that changed how teams played over ten seasons (rest, back-to-backs, overtime, close-win letdowns, blowout wins); the rest tested at zero and stay off. Checked on last season (each night from that season's earlier games only): team scores, player points and threes landed where the sims said as often as they should. Early in the season a player's rates start from his own last season and lean on this season's games as they come in (about four games' worth). A play is his main line's side with 4+ points of edge at the best price; the Ladder tag shows a rung on the same side with more. The books still predict the final score a little better than the sims do; a play is where the two disagree by enough.
