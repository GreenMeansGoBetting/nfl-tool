Every Game Played 10,000 Times

Each game is played out possession by possession from this season's games: tonight's players, who shoots, from where, against this defense. The spots that proved real over ten seasons tilt the dice. The books' line is never an input; it shows up only at the end, as the price.

1 · Who's On The Floor2 · Who Shoots3 · From Where4 · Does It Go In5 · Repeat 10,000×

One game, a quarter at a time: the two teams' pace sets the possessions. Each possession goes to one of the five on the floor, by his minutes and how much he shoots when he's out there. His play is a shot from a zone (rim, short, mid-range, corner three, above-the-break three), a trip to the line or a turnover, by his own mix tilted toward what this defense gives up. Whether it goes in is his make rate there times what this defense allows there. A missed shot can come back as an offensive rebound. Down the stretch a blowout sends the starters to the bench, and a lead shrinks a little (the team ahead eases off). Every player also gets a minutes night and a shooting night, so the spread of outcomes is a real game's. Injuries and starters: a player listed Out leaves his minutes to his own position first; a player in the NBA's starting five tonight plays starter minutes. The dials are the spots that changed how teams played over ten seasons (rest, back-to-backs, overtime, close-win letdowns, blowout wins); the rest tested at zero and stay off. Checked on last season (each night from that season's earlier games only): team scores, player points and threes landed where the sims said as often as they should. This season only: early on, a player's rates lean on the league's numbers for his position this season, less as his own games come in. The books still predict the final score a little better than the sims do; a play is where the two disagree by enough.
