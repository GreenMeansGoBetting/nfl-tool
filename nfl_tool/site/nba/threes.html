Projections In Testing Until Mid-November

We can predict how many threes a player will try, but not how many he'll make. Every projection is graded against the tip-off line, live.

Why tries come first: on last season's replay, how many threes a player tries tonight was predictable; whether they go in mostly wasn't. So the projection is his tries x his make rate, and the make rate barely moves (his season, pulled 200 tries toward the league's). What moved tries on last season's replay: his minutes (a dip over the last 5 bounces back), teammates listed Out (their shots go to role players, not the star), both teams' pace, how many threes this defense allows, how many of them are catch-and-shoot (help defense leaving shooters), his team being the underdog (trailing teams shoot more), and a cold or hot last 5 (a cold run trims his tries a little; it doesn't change his make rate). What didn't: corner vs above-the-break threes, what % a defense allows, free-throw %, back-to-backs. The ladder: a night's threes swing wider than a plain average says, so 1+ hits a little less often and 3+ / 4+ more often than most expect. The edge is our chance of the Over minus the books' no-vig chance. Early in the season the projection leans on the books and moves toward our number as games come in. About 2,000 graded lines (mid-November) is when the record starts to mean something.

L5 / L10 / Season / H-A / vs: how often he's gone over tonight's line in his last 5, last 10, the season, tonight's home or road games, and against tonight's opponent ("x of y"). Min: season minutes, last 5 under it. 3PA: threes tried a game this season, last 5 under it. Tags read both sides: 3s Allowed = this defense gives up a lot of threes and he shoots them; Tough 3s D = it gives up few; Help D = its threes allowed are mostly catch-and-shoot and he lives on them; Underdog = his team is getting 6+ (trailing teams shoot more); Usage + = rotation teammates listed Out tonight; Min ↑ / ↓ = his last 5 games' minutes moved 3+; Cold Run / Hot Run = his last 5 made well under / over his own rate.
