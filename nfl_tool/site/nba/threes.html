Projections In Testing Until Mid-November

We can predict how many threes a player will try, but not how many he'll make. Every projection is graded against the tip-off line, live.

Why tries come first: on last season's replay, how many threes a player tries tonight was predictable; whether they go in mostly wasn't. So the projection is his tries x his make rate, and the make rate barely moves (his season, pulled 200 tries toward the league's). What moved tries on last season's replay: his minutes (a dip over the last 5 bounces back), teammates listed Out (their shots go to role players, not the star), both teams' pace, how many threes this defense allows, how many of them are catch-and-shoot (help defense leaving shooters), his team being the underdog (trailing teams shoot more), and a cold or hot last 5 (a cold run trims his tries a little; it doesn't change his make rate). What didn't: corner vs above-the-break threes, what % a defense allows, free-throw %, back-to-backs. The ladder: a night's threes swing wider than a plain average says, so 1+ hits a little less often and 3+ / 4+ more often than most expect. The play is the side our chance beats its best price by more, graded at the best price at tip-off. Early in the season the projection leans on the books and moves toward our number as games come in. About 2,000 graded lines (mid-November) is when the record starts to mean something.
