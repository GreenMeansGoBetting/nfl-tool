How the number is made, and how far to trust it

The King of the Crease saves model, all from this season's games: a league-average start, times how much the other team shoots, times how many shots his own team gives up, times home ice and rest, times his save rate (a small piece: over a handful of starts it is mostly luck). Shot volume is read mostly from shots on goal and partly from shot attempts, with ten games of a league-average team mixed in. Proj is his saves tonight; the chance of the over allows for the 1 start in 20 that ends early. Fair is the price the chance of the play is worth.

The goalie is the expected starter: the NHL's projected lineup and the goalie Novig prices, checked against each other. A check mark means the two agree; it is never a confirmation (starters are official at warmups). If a different goalie starts, books void the bet and the record leaves it out.

What the replay showed (5,034 starts over 2024-25 and 2025-26, each projected only from that season's games before it): the two teams' shot volume is nearly all of it (each moves a projection about 6% either way); home ice and rest about 3%; his own save rate about 1%. The chances were honest from 20 saves to 40. Early in a season it knows little: in the first two weeks it missed by 5.4 saves a start, barely better than calling every goalie average, and it reaches full strength around game 20.

What it has not shown: that it beats the line. There is no free history of saves lines, so the page keeps its own record (YTD Model Performance), with how far each price moved from the first one the page saw (closing line value). Novig's saves book is thin: often one side has no price. A gap of 12+ points, or a model 4+ saves away from what the line says, is set apart as check: more often a starter or news the market knows than a gift.
