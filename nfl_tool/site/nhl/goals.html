How the number is made, and how far to trust it

His expected goals a minute × his minutes tonight × tonight's matchup, all from this season's games. Expected goals (xG) is what a shot is worth from where and how it was taken: distance, angle, the part of the ice, shot type, rebound, rush, the power play, and the off wing (a right-handed shot from the left side, the one-timer side). The table is fitted on this season's shots every time the page is built. His rate is read separately at even strength and on the power play, each times his minutes there; a small sample is mixed with an average skater who plays his minutes. Model is his chance of 1 or more goals.

What moves it (the columns on the right; green = more goals for him): the other defense's expected goals allowed (Opp), tonight's goalie against what his shots should have allowed (Goalie: the NHL's projected starter, else the team's busiest; a projection, never confirmed), his own goals against his expected goals pulled hard toward average (Finish: most of a hot start is luck), even strength and power play read apart (EV/PP), Style (shooters who live in the low slot ran a little under in the replay, and off-wing shots ran a little over), and home ice and rest.

Zone Match (blue tag): 35% more of his shots from the low slot or the slot than his position takes from there, against a defense in the league's top 10 for shots allowed from that area. It is shown and graded in the record, but it is not in the number: in two full seasons of replays, tagged players scored more often than others, but no more than the model already expected. A defense's low-slot goals allowed after a handful of games told nothing about the rest of its season; its low-slot shots allowed did, so the tag reads shots.

No odds: Novig has no anytime price for him yet. Every player is still listed with the model's fair price, to shop elsewhere; prices fill in through the day as the page refreshes. First seen (in a row's breakdown) is the first price the page saw. Every play is graded at the price at puck drop (the last refresh, up to 30 minutes before). The record also shows how far Novig's own price moved from its first look to puck drop (closing line value) as context; the first look is usually before lineups.

What the replays showed (2024-25 to fit, 2025-26 to check, every game projected only from that season's games before it): the chances were honest (players given 30 to 35% scored 33%; the model's top 2% were given 40% and scored 43%), and the model beat a player's goals a game clearly. Expected goals is only a little better than his shots times the league's shooting %: most of a goal is luck.

What it has not shown: that it beats the price. There is no free history of anytime prices, so the page keeps its own record (YTD Model Performance). A gap of 12+ points (check) is more often something the market knows than a gift, and so is a player moved down a line tonight.
