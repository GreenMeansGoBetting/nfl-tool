How the number is made, and how far to trust it

His expected goals + his expected assists, all from this season's games. The goals are the Goals page's number. The assists are the expected goals his teammates make while he is on the ice (from the league's shift charts), times the share of their goals he has assisted on, read separately at even strength and on the power play, times his minutes tonight. Chances instead of goals, because assists swing on luck: a small sample is mixed with an average player who plays his minutes. Fair is the price his chance of the play is worth.

What moves it (green = more points for him): the other defense's expected goals allowed (Opp, the biggest piece), tonight's goalie (Goalie: the NHL's projected starter, else the team's busiest; a projection, never confirmed), his line tonight, and home ice and rest.

Linemates (blue): tonight's projected linemates against the ones he has played with, by their points a minute. It is shown and graded in the record, but it is not in the number: in two seasons of replays it pointed the right way, but too little to prove once his line and minutes were counted.

What the replays showed (2024-25 to fit, 2025-26 to check, every game projected only from that season's games before it): the chances were honest (players given 45 to 55% got a point 50% of the time, 55 to 65% got one 60%), and the model beat a player's points a game. Counting a second assist for less added nothing.

What it has not shown: that it beats the price. There is no free history of points prices, so the page keeps its own record (YTD Model Performance), including how far Novig's own price moved toward the play from the first look to puck drop (closing line value). On the first six nights the market's own price predicted a point better than the model, so a play needs all three: the 0.5 line, an edge of 6+ points, and not against tonight's line change (those went 42-57; the 1.5 lines 8-20). An edge of 25+ points is Model Unsure: the model is missing something.
